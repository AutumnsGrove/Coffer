# Product

## Register

product

## Users

One person (Autumn), on her own tailnet. She opens Coffer about once a month, on a laptop at a desk in the
evening, to download two bank CSVs and drop them in. She then reads the month, sometimes on her phone on
the couch. There is no second user and no onboarding to strangers.

The job on any given visit is one of two things: get this month's files in safely (import), or understand
where the money went (read). Everything else is occasional upkeep.

## Product Purpose

A private, self-hosted ledger that turns a monthly bank CSV into a readable picture of the month, kept
forever on her own machine. Success is a month she can read in two minutes and trust to the cent, and an
app that tells her when she has forgotten to import, because forgetting is the real failure mode.

## Brand Personality

Warm, personal, exact. A well-kept household ledger rather than a fintech product. It speaks plainly, never
nags, and never congratulates or scolds her about her spending.

## Anti-references

- Mint and YNAB: cheerful gamified colour, progress rings, budgeting nag.
- Crypto and neon dashboards: dark glow, gradients, a giant hero number.
- Bank-portal corporate: navy and grey enterprise chrome, tiny dense panels.
- Card-grid SaaS templates: everything wrapped in identical rounded cards.
- Anything that forces horizontal scrolling to read a table.

## Design Principles

1. **A ledger, not a dashboard.** Ruled lines, aligned figures and generous rhythm carry the structure.
   Surfaces are paper; boxes are the exception.
2. **Numbers are the interface.** Amounts are right-aligned in tabular figures and are always the
   easiest thing on the screen to read and compare. Nothing decorates them.
3. **Colour is state, never decoration.** Amber means overdue, slate means transfer, red means a rise or a
   rejected row. Everything else is warm ink on paper, so a coloured thing is always news.
4. **Show the risky moment honestly.** The import preview exists to catch a wrong sign or a wrong
   account before anything is written. Design that screen for checking, not for speed.
5. **Read on a phone, import on a laptop.** Reading screens are phone-first and never need horizontal
   scrolling; Import is laptop-first and merely works on a phone.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA contrast for all text in both light and dark themes (the page follows the system setting). Tap
targets at least 44px on a phone. Meaning is never carried by colour alone: every coloured state also has
a word or a sign (a minus, "Transfer", "Overdue"). Visible focus rings. Honour reduced motion.
